### His take on Founder's Institute
I’m not a fan of the FI model. For many reasons.

1. It follows the ideate, build mvp, launch and scale model – An almost surefire way to waste you capital and fail.
	1. They are doing things at scale, many chapters and many cohorts.
	2. As a result, they have some good success stories – is that a result of skill or throwing enough mud?

2. They use poorly screened volunteer mentors – the experience depends on who’s in charge locally.
	1. Some chapters I hear really good things about
	2. Other chapters I hear really bad things about
3. Mentors take equity in the startup – making it difficult to remove them if there is a conflict.

Our key selling points are;
1. Targeting the right founders.
2. Educating them on the importance of;
	1. A very disciplined definition of PMF.
	2. Then sowing them a very clear path to get there.
	3. Holding their hand all the way, and constantly course correcting for market feedback and unknown, unknowns.
3. We can’t guarantee success.  We can guarantee they won’t get buyers remorse – we’re the very best way a founder can spend their time and capital to reach success.

Re: Targeting
- We don’t target inexperienced founders.  They will be drawn to the FI promise and price.  I find this a bit predatory actually.
- We target more experienced founders that understand it’s very easy to waste money on the wrong things.  It’s why we target;
	1. Corporate leavers, particularly those with senior management experience.
	2. Service business owners that want to pivot to scalable product companies.
	3. And unfortunately people who have been burnt by spending on approaches that don’t work, like FI.
