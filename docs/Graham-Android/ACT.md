**Stance**: nurturing grandparent, wise elder, enabling to see.  
**Essence**: Seer and creator: creating solutions to problems others cannot see. Hence my stance, my job is to grandparent them to see.
I'm --- this --- as a gift to Life.
# 1 Defusion
Shift from trying to find the right thought, from trying to meditate between warring children, to accepting that Alfred is doing his thing, watching the thoughts with curiosity, saying "Thanks, but I've got this." Accept that thinking is chaotic always, stop trying to impose order, focus on what's useful to move forwards. 
Key signs of fusion: 
* Thoughts are predictable, have stayed the same for so long they're part of me. 
* I've disappeared into my thoughts, emerging later as if from a reverie. 
* My thoughts are highly competitive and evaluative, Vs. merely observing. 
* Over busy mode, default mode network, lots of contradictions, self-admonitions, and rules. Yearning for coherence and understanding 
* Leads to trying to impose my idea of order. 
* Leads to living in my head. 
* With a focus on life as a problem to solve, and on literal coherence 
* And a stance of being right. (vs. my strategic stance of nurturing grandparent / wise elder enabling to see what's hidden). 
 
Pivot to learning what works and 
* Functional coherence (focus on those thoughts useful for living in accordance with my values, letting go of focus on those that are unhelpful. i.e., my stance and essence and intent.) 
* Increasing my cognitive flexibility; to get 
* Greater wisdom and peace of mind.
### Practices
1. Deliberately Disobeying an I can't, I don't deserve statement. Do in small chunks, then Ground Pattern. Simple practice: walk around room while saying I can't walk around the room. 
2. Listening politely to Alfred. Practice stalking, or the ACT placing each thought on a leaf on a stream. 
3. Answering Alfred back appreciating his intention to solve my problems and protect me. 
4. Giving myself compassion, not ridicule, abuse, nor shame; where it came from as a child. (Treat myself from my chosen stance as well! Grandparent myself!)

# 2: Take Perspective
Stop Yearning for esteem and recognition from others, where  
- Not being special is the issue, 
- leading to defending, lying, and hiding; 
- with a focus on my story, comparison to others, and aloneness,   

Start taking greater perspective,   
- taking sense of self, 
- with a focus on awareness and community, 
- resulting in greater connection to myself and others as whole and aware beings. (S2 to S3 to S4 to S5)
### Practices
1. I am +ve... I am +ve... I am -ve ... Then interrogate. Always, everywhere, everyone? Would all agree? See as comparatives Vs an FoR. Notice fusion and identity story, then add: ... or not! Use defusion skills of merely noticing, refuse to buy into one version of myself (S5). Cross out, asking who I'd be without that. Rewrite each "I am ... " to: "I feel/think ....  " and then add NVC context qualifications: "When [situation] and I [behaviour] then [how I think and feel].
2. Rewrite my story. Keep same facts and reactions, but rewrite the meaning making.
3. Catch self awareness on the fly and ask who is noticing that.
4. Distinguish between awareness itself and content of awareness. And use pattern of "what else".

# 3 : Acceptance, Learning from pain
Pivot from yearning to feel good,   
- leading to avoidance of negative feelings (then avoiding all positive feelings as well) 
- with a focus on getting rid of pain and discomfort, leading to disconnection from my body, 
- leading to emotional dysfunction.
To accepting feeling means I'm alive and care,    
- so growing greater emotional flexibility and function by connecting with my body and focussing on learning from pain, and on growing; 
- leading to a greater willingness to just feel.
### Practices
1. Yes-ing. Practice saying yes to things, feelings, thoughts I'd usually say no to. Then ground pattern.
2. Caring. Honour the experience, listen carefully, don't avoid, appreciate Alfred caring for me.
3. Wider viewing. Zoom out, look from a wiser and wider future, what can I learn from this. As a hero's journey, what will this enable me to do?
4. Oppositing. Do the opposite to what my mind says to avoid / do.

# 4: Presence, living in the now
Pivot from: yearning for orientation  
- and then orientating myself by ruminating on the past that got me here and the futures I either want to avoid having, or aspire to have; 
- how and why becomes my issue. 
- My focus is limited to my concept of my past and my future, not my actual past and future. 
- So anchored in then and there, lots of worry and rumination.
To: flexible, fluid, voluntary attention on the here and now, and useful noticing,  
- leading to greater attention to my present internal and external environment.
- Acceptance of what I am feeling and what is around me and my work, rather than avoiding feelings or thoughts. 
- Not taking Alfred so seriously. 
- Not trying to find, in yet another new method, the solution to problem X.
### Practices
1. Meditating. Simple, like breathing or stalking.
2. Attention, Broader and narrower. Focus on a leaf, then branch, then tree, then forest, then planet.
3. Spaces, relationships. Opening my focus. Focus on an object, then on the relationship to our space between all other objects.

# 5: Value - caring by choice
Pivot from   
- Yearning for meaning and self-direction 
- leads to pursue wants and shoulds, a focus on acquisition and compliance, being programmed, self-righteous and seeking gratification. 
- Resulting in emptiness or insensitivity. 
Pivot to  
- a focus on chosen qualities of self-determined action, 
- leading to increased appreciation and motivation, 
- resulting in greater meaning and purpose by choice. 
Pain is a flashlight if we know where to point the beam. Values writing. 
### Practices
1. Drawing out sweetness. Recall a sweet event, flesh out in an essay. Who else, what was I doing, feeling, thinking? What does this tell me I care about?
2. Flipping pain into purpose. Identify all barriers to expressing my purpose, then ask: "what would I have to _not_ care about for it to not hurt?" Then write down - to repeat each time - "If in (situation) I feel / think / XXX (...) remember it means that I care about (...)"
